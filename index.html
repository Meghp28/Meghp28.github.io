Multi-Site Inventory System

Python · Streamlit · PostgreSQL (Supabase) · psycopg2 · Git

What it is

I started with a simple question: how do small business owners who can't squeeze QuickBooks into their budgets keep track of their inventory? There seemed to be no clear answer to this question. You either pay huge amounts of money for inventory management subscriptions or track everything by hand in an Excel workbook. To solve this, I built a web app that lets you to see inventory at multiple sites and to log sales, transfers between sites, and restocks. On top of that, users can run reports right in the app, so they don't have to export data to another program.

What was hard

Multiple locations means every unit has to be counted exactly once. A transfer from the warehouse to an Amazon site is a decrease in one place and an increase in another, and if those two writes ever disagree, the numbers drift. The other hard part was the past: a business owner may have years of history living in Excel, and the app isn't useful until that history is in the database with the right dates.

What I did

  • Built the app from scratch in Streamlit, starting on SQLite and moving to a hosted PostgreSQL database on Supabase.
  • Modeled sales, transfers, adjustments, and restock history, with dates entered by the user so late entries land on the right day.
  • Wrote import scripts that loaded the historical Excel data for sales, transfers, and adjustments, then moved the app onto production data.
  • Debugged a stuck database lock by finding the blocking session and ending it with pg_terminate_backend().
  • Found a database password that had been exposed and flagged it for rotation. That was my first real security incident, and a small one.

Visual Parts Counter

Python · OpenCV · Flask · centroid tracking

What it is

A camera system that counts parts as they move along an assembly line, with a Flask page that shows the live count.

What was hard

Spotting a part in one frame is the easy half. The hard half is counting it once. The same part shows up in dozens of frames, so the system has to recognize "that's the one I already saw" as it moves. I used centroid tracking to follow each object across frames and count it when it crosses a line. Accuracy is still the open problem: parts that touch or overlap can merge into one blob. Getting accuracy high enough takes a very large amount of training data.

What I did

  • Built the detection pipeline in OpenCV and the tracking and counting logic on top of it.
  • Served the feed and the count through a small Flask app.
  • Tuned it for accuracy on the real line, and I'm now testing a fine-tuned YOLOv8 model to replace the classic detection step.
  • It is still a work in progress.

Real-Time ASL Translator

Python · webcam video · hand-landmark tracking · classifier

What it is

A sponsored project that reads American Sign Language from a webcam and turns it into on-screen text in real time. The goal is to make a conversation between a signer and someone who doesn't know ASL possible without an interpreter in the room.

What was hard

The requirements changed partway through. We started with a translator that reads signs from a webcam, and then the sponsors asked for a video-calling interface that translates while the call is happening. That meant the recognition had to keep up with a live video stream instead of working on its own.

What I did

  • Used hand-tracking landmarks from the webcam feed as the input to a classifier that predicts which sign is being made.
  • Showed the predicted signs as text on screen, live.
  • Adapted the project when the sponsors' requirements shifted to a video-calling interface with live translation.
  • It is still a work in progress.

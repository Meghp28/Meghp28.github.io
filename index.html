Multi-Site Inventory System

Python · Streamlit · PostgreSQL (Supabase) · psycopg2 · Git

What it is

I started with a simple question, how do small business owners, who may not be able to squeeze Quickbooks into their budgets, keep a track of their inventory? There seemed to be no clear answer to this question. You either have to pay extraordinary amounts of money for subscriptions for inventory management software or do it manually in an excel workbook. To try and solve this I built a web app the allows you to see inventory at multiple sites and to log sales, transfers between sites, and restocks. On top of this, the user can also run reports for convinience so they don't have to export any data to another app.

What was hard

Multiple locations means every unit has to be counted exactly once. A transfer from the warehouse to an Amazon site is a decrease in one place and an increase in another, and if those two writes ever disagree, the numbers drift. The other hard part was the past: say the business owner wants years of history that lived in Excel, and the app wouldn't be useful until that history was in the database with the right dates.

What I did

  • Built the app from scratch in Streamlit, starting on SQLite and moving to a hosted PostgreSQL database on Supabase.
  • Modeled sales, transfers, adjustments, and restock history, with dates entered by the user so late entries land on the right day.
  • Wrote import scripts that loaded the historical Excel data for sales, transfers, and adjustments, then moved the app onto production data.
  • Debugged a stuck database lock by finding the blocking session and ending it with pg_terminate_backend().
  • Found a database password that had been exposed and flagged it for rotation. That was my first real security incident, and a small one.

Visual Parts Counter

Python · OpenCV · Flask · centroid tracking

What it is

A camera system that counts parts as they move along an assembly line, with a Flask page that shows the live count.

What was hard

Spotting a part in one frame is the easy half. The hard half is counting it once. The same part shows up in dozens of frames, so the system has to recognize "that's the one I already saw" as it moves. I used centroid tracking to follow each object across frames and count it when it crosses a line. Accuracy is still the open problem: parts that touch or overlap can merge into one blob. The training required for the accuracy to be high enough is extremely large.

What I did

  • Built the detection pipeline in OpenCV and the tracking and counting logic on top of it.
  • Served the feed and the count through a small Flask app.
  • Tuned it for accuracy on the real line, and I'm now testing a fine-tuned YOLOv8 model to replace the classic detection step.
  • It is still a work in progress.
